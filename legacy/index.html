The Problem

THE SIX-PAIR LIE.

The shoe industry sells specialisation. One shoe for running. One for the gym. One for the commute. One for the weekend. The rack grows. The wallet shrinks.

In India, the average buyer owns 2.3 pairs of shoes. Not because they lack aspiration — because they lack space, because they lack waste-tolerance, because they were raised to respect an object that lasts.

The market doesn't need more shoes. It needs fewer, better ones that do more.

"The most admired object in an Indian house is never the newest one."

— Consumer insight, 2024 fieldwork

The Position

ONE PAIR. THREE USES.

IT'S THE THIRD THAT CLOSES THE SALE.

A

WHAT THEY CAME FOR

The primary use case. The reason they walked into the store or typed the search. This is table stakes.

B

WHAT THEY'LL ALSO DO IN IT

The adjacent activity. The thing they'd do in the shoe anyway, whether designed for it or not. We design for it.

C

WHAT THEY DIDN'T KNOW THEY WERE BUYING

The invisible feature. The thing that makes them keep the shoe two years longer. This is where brand lives.

Every shoe in the line is briefed against A/B/C. If we can't fill C, we don't ship it. The ₹2,000 shoe does three jobs. The ₹4,500 shoe does three jobs better. Neither does one job only.

PHOTOGRAPHY RULES

01The shoe is never the hero of the frame. The activity is.

02No shoe is ever clean. If it's new, dirty it first.

03No white backgrounds. No floating product. No void.

04Hands are always in frame — tying, adjusting, carrying.

05Ground is always visible. Context is mandatory.

06If the logo is visible, it's incidental, not composed.

07One shoe per image. Never a pair. Pairs are retail. Singles are editorial.

Lotto is shot in daylight. one8 is shot in the dark.
